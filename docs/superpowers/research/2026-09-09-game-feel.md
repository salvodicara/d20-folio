# Game feel without videogame graphics — the evidence base for d20 Folio

Claude, 2026-09-09, for block PD. The owner's brief: the experience is "troppo fredda, distaccata e
amministrativa, come una pratica alle poste" (`docs/program/DECISIONS.md`, 2026-09-09). This
document answers one question with sources: what makes a browser tool with sheets and controls feel
like a game, and which of those mechanisms are behaviour, flow, copy and feedback — the half that
is not a graphic skin. Each mechanism ends in a rule a test or a screenshot review can check.
Nothing here decides a colour, a layout or a typeface; where a rule touches one, it says "reserved
to Astra" and states only the constraint.

Baldur's Gate 3 is abbreviated BG3 below. Evidence files: [`2026-09-09-rule-30/reference-game-feel.md`](2026-09-09-rule-30/reference-game-feel.md)
(`RGF`, ninety-six URLs, labelled official / talk / research / wiki / press / forum / unverified),
the four job references in the same folder (`RC`, `RCH`, `RL`, `RT`), the PD research
([`2026-09-09-pd-bg3-dndbeyond-research.md`](2026-09-09-pd-bg3-dndbeyond-research.md), `PD`) and
the [prior-research index](2026-09-09-rule-30/prior-research-index.md) (`PRI`). Strings marked
`RW` come from Claude's runtime walk of `v2` @ `aac3e5fe` (evidence folder
`~/Workspace/Codex/d20-folio-pd-rule30-audit-evidence/runtime-walk.md`); `MW` from the lab walk.

## 1. The finding in one paragraph

The same complaint exists in the market, verbatim: Foundry's best-selling character-creation module
sells itself as making players feel they are "stepping into your world rather than filling out a
tax form" (RGF §1, official package page), and the 2026 D&D Beyond relayout thread calls the result
"sterile … spreadsheet with a dragon logo" (RGF §8, forum). The replies in that thread show that
better organisation alone does not answer it. What answers it, with evidence, is a short list of
mechanisms that the best products share and that d20 Folio's shipped surfaces lack: a sentence of
world beside every step, the character's name in every title after it is typed, the numeric
consequence before the click and the same number in the log, a copy register whose subject is the
player or the character rather than the record, a visible settle after every commit, and a skip for
every ritual. None of them is art.

## 2. Mechanisms, evidence, rules

### 2.1 Narrative framing of steps

**Evidence.** Baldur's Gate 3 narrates the same step differently per identity ("17 completely
different narration styles", press, RGF §1) and renders a check as die → modifiers → verdict, so a
failing roll visibly becomes a success (RGF §1). Disco Elysium makes a stat a voice; Hades writes
its codex in a character's hand; Crusader Kings 3 delivers a state change as scene text plus named
choices, never as a toast (RGF §1). In the tabletop market: Roll20's Charactermancer pairs each step
with "a passage from the rulebook on the right" (official blog); D&D Beyond's Quick Builder brief
says "Lead with iconic D&D art, not walls of text" and "Provide easy default selections"
(official); Foundry's Originate frames each Class/Race/Background with art or video (RGF §1).

**Our surfaces today.** The creation heading "Bring a character to life" and the aside "The story
takes shape" are the only framing lines in the runtime; every step below them is a field label
(RW §Creation). The mock frames every route ("Il guado è alle vostre spalle. La città non ha ancora
aperto le sue porte."; "La strada davanti a voi è ancora intatta."; "Un momento di tregua") (MW).

**Rule G1.** Every multi-step flow and every domain root shows one sentence of in-world framing
beside the control, naming the character or the world and the consequence ("Marco learns Shield.
It costs a reaction."). A step or a root with no framing sentence fails the review. Reserved to
Astra: where the sentence sits.

### 2.2 Identity-first moments

**Evidence.** Larian's telemetry: 94 % made a custom character and 8,196 years were spent in the
creator (press, RGF §2); Pokémon and Zelda ask for the name first and every NPC uses it (wikis,
RGF §2); D&D Beyond's fastest path still requires the name ("Pick your species, class, and name",
official, RGF §2); its portrait is available early through the builder header (official support,
RGF §2). No controlled study on name-first ordering exists (PD E4 §5); the convergence is the
evidence.

**Our surfaces today.** The name is the first field, but the headings never use it: "Character",
"Your new character", "This declaration needs review", "No characters here" (RW; CI §2.2).

**Rule G2.** Name (and a portrait or a chosen placeholder) is captured before any numeric field;
from then on every title, confirmation, error and empty state that concerns the character uses the
name. A screen that says "Character" or "this record" where a name exists fails. Data seam: the
name is already in `CreationDraft.name`; interpolation is an i18n change.

### 2.3 Live consequence previews before commit

**Evidence.** Fire Emblem's forecast shows damage, hit and crit before the attack (RGF §3); Into
the Breach gives "perfect information" so "every death felt like the player's own fault" (press
interview, GDC talk, RGF §3); Slay the Spire shows the enemy's intent in damage bands (wiki);
XCOM shows the hit percentage and its designer notes players read it "as a promise" (press);
Baldur's Gate 3 shows "% chance to hit" on hover, and a tabletop mod replaces it with the d20
minimum because table players want the table number (RGF §3); D&D Beyond auto-prepares doubled
crit damage on a natural 20 (official, RGF §3). The mock already does this: "Danni: 5 + 3 = 8 ·
Capitano: 28 → 20 PF · Azione: disponibile → usata · Quadrelli: 20 → 19" before "Applica attacco",
and "Anteprima: 38 → 35 PF · 5 → 0 temporanei" before applying damage (MW §4.1, §2.1).

**Our surfaces today.** Creation shows Maximum HP and Gold only after a class exists; AC, speed,
skills, saves and slots wait for the review page (`CreationWizard.tsx:299-327`; PD E5 §3).

**Rule G3.** Every commit control (create, level up, prepare, equip, spend points, apply damage,
apply a decision) shows the numeric delta before the click, and the log line after the click
carries the same numbers. No preview fails; a preview that differs from the receipt is a bug with
a test. Data seam: `forecastCreation`/`speculate` in `src/lib/views/creation-forecast.ts` (this
task) for creation; the P03 receipt model for play.

### 2.4 Language and tone: game register versus administrative register

**Evidence.** The neutral baseline is Shopify Polaris ("start with a strong verb", active voice,
grade-7 reading level), Mailchimp ("it's always more important to be clear than entertaining") and
GOV.UK (second person, active voice, ≤ 25 words) — official style guides, RGF §4. GOV.UK is also the
counter-example: obey all of it and a form still reads like the post office, because what GOV.UK
forbids (metaphor, character, world) is what games add. Game copy: Baldur's Gate 3 tooltips are
second person, present tense, mechanic plus number; Hades' codex has an author and an addressee;
Darkest Dungeon narrates a status change as an aphorism; Elden Ring's message system proves that
fixed templates with slot words keep flavour translatable (RGF §4). NN/g on empty states: status,
learning cue, direct pathway (research, RGF §4). Foundry's dnd5e removed "question-mark phrasing"
from its rest dialogs (pull request, RT (f)): controls are imperatives.

**The register diff (derived, RGF §4).** Administrative = noun phrases, passive or impersonal,
the system as subject ("Record saved"), no addressee. Game = verb first, second person, present
tense, the character or the world as subject, the consequence in the sentence.

**Rule G4 — the four checks, applied to every user-visible string.** (1) The subject is "you" or
the character's name; (2) present tense, verb-first on controls; (3) the consequence or the next
action is in the sentence; (4) no system nouns as subject — "record", "entry", "item", "data",
"form", "declaration", "operation", "receipt" unless the sentence is about a receipt the player
asked for. Empty states carry a learning cue and an in-world reason the container is empty. Errors
keep the GOV.UK shape (specific, short, blame-free) and add at most one clause of world.

### 2.5 Feedback and micro-rewards

**Evidence.** Steve Swink's definition: polish emphasises interactions "without changing the
underlying simulation" (book, RGF §5) — juice must never change the roll. Vlambeer's "Art of
Screenshake" and "Juice It or Lose It" (talks) stack small feedbacks on one action; Game Developer's
counter-voice warns against juicing everything (RGF §5). Dice: D&D Beyond's shared 3D dice are seen
"in real time by their party" and premium sets carry crit rune rings and a nat-1 mimic (official,
forum); Roll20's 3D dice are "a real physics simulation"; Foundry's Dice So Nice has Normal/2x/3x
speed and a 2000 ms auto-hide; Owlbear's 2.0 dice use "a deterministic physics engine" so everyone
sees the same roll, with a hidden-roll eye toggle (official, RGF §5). Baldur's Gate 3 lands the
bonuses after the d20 so the build is seen rescuing the roll (press, RGF §1); its forum asks for a
dice-animation skip (RGF §5). NN/g: feedback by urgency — badges passive, toasts with undo, modals
only when action is required (PRI §21).

**Our surfaces today.** No toast, no motion, no sound on any commit in the runtime; the only
feedback is the text "Draft saved on this device" (RW §Cross-cutting). The mock has a 4500 ms
`role=status` toast and health bars that recolour at 50 % / 20 % (MW §0).

**Rule G5.** Every roll has a visible settle (the result appears after the trigger, not with it),
a distinct natural-20 and natural-1 state, and a sound that can be muted; the value is fixed before
the animation starts, so log and animation can never disagree (golden rule 32, `src/lib/dice.ts`).
Every commit outside play (save, seal, add, join) gets a passive confirmation with undo where undo
exists, never a modal. Every juice element is removable by one setting without changing a number.
Reserved to Astra: the form of the settle and the celebration.

### 2.6 Sound and motion budgets

**Evidence.** Material 3 duration tokens: 50–200 ms for feedback, 250–400 ms for dialogs and
panels, 450–600 ms for layout, 700–1000 ms full-screen only (official spec, values confirmed via a
token mirror because the page is client-rendered, RGF §6); Microsoft WinUI: 83 / 167 / 250 ms
(official); Apple HIG: "Add motion purposefully", "Make motion optional", avoid motion on frequent
interactions (official, quotes from excerpts); WCAG 2.3.3: interaction-triggered motion can be
disabled unless essential (standard, RGF §6). Game side: Slay the Spire's Fast Mode roughly doubles
animation speed; Civilization's Quick Combat; Dice So Nice 2x/3x plus 2000 ms auto-hide; BG3 has no native combat-speed setting and players install speed mods (RGF §6). The prior
research already fixed ≤ 200–300 ms ease-out, transform/opacity, interruptible, never on keyboard
(PRI rule 21).

**Rule G6 — the budget table.** Every animation in the app is tagged with one of four budgets, and
the tag is checkable in code review:

| Budget      | Use                                            | Ceiling | Skippable | Reduced motion       |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ------- | --------- | -------------------- |
| feedback    | press, toggle, hover, focus                    | 200 ms  | n/a       | keep                 |
| state       | panel, dialog, tab, list reorder               | 300 ms  | n/a       | 0 ms, same end state |
| settle      | a roll, a receipt landing, an HP bar moving    | 1000 ms | yes       | 0 ms                 |
| celebration | natural 20 / 1, level gained, death save, rest | 1500 ms | yes       | 0 ms                 |

Nothing blocks input longer than a settle; a celebration fires at most once per event type per
session; `prefers-reduced-motion` collapses the last three to zero with the same end state, and an
in-app "fast" preference does the same for people without the OS flag (the owner's 2026-09-07
decision forbids a switch that disables the experience; a speed preference is not that switch — the
distinction to record in the settings copy). Sound follows the same table and is muted by one
control (Roll20 Jukebox per-user master volume, RT (e)).

### 2.7 Expert bypass

**Evidence.** Baldur's Gate 3 pre-fills abilities per class and offers "Use Recommended" to restore
the spread after edits (guide, RGF §7); D&D Beyond offers Standard / Quick Build / Randomize and a
"Show Help Text" toggle in the builder header, on by default, remembered (official support, RGF
§7); Hades' God Mode is framed as a gift, and no story is gated behind refusing it (press
interview); Slay the Spire and Civilization ship global speed toggles, not per-instance prompts;
Foundry experts bind roll modes to hotkeys (official package, RGF §7). The quick path is a minority
path everywhere (7 % in Baldur's Gate 3, "doesn't see much use" at D&D Beyond, PD §9.1).

**Rule G7.** Every ritual (a framing sentence, a settle, a celebration, a help panel) has (a) a
one-input skip, (b) a remembered global switch, (c) on any step with more than three choices, a
"Recommended" one-click default that stays editable. A ritual without its skip, or a preference
that must be reset next session, fails. Data seam: `applyRecommendedBuild` (this task) and the
`FolioAccount` preference store (P02).

### 2.8 Tabletop rituals reproduced digitally

**Evidence.** Roll modes: Foundry public / private-GM / blind / self with "Reveal to Everyone" and
"Make Private" after the fact (official API and article, RGF §9, RT (c)); Roll20 `/gmroll`, and 3D
dice suppressed for secret rolls "to avoid giving away that a hidden roll took place" (official, RC
§Job B); D&D Beyond's Game Log shipped without hidden rolls and got Everyone / Self / DM two years
later (changelog, RT failure 2). "Roll for initiative": D&D Beyond Maps' "Start Combat" button
(official); "You may now level up": D&D Beyond's arrow and `(!)` markers make the threshold an
invitation, not an automatic change (forum, RCH (d)); crits: D&D Beyond auto-prepares doubled
damage (official).

**Rule G8.** Every roll carries one of the four visibility modes, defaulting to public for players
and to the DM's remembered choice for the DM (the resetting default is the documented failure, RC
failure list); the sheet marks "level available" as an invitation the player accepts, never as an
automatic change; combat starts from a control labelled with the table phrase; crit and fumble
states come from the rules, not from a purchase.

## 3. Do not copy — documented failures

Each item is a failure documented by its own users or vendor, not a preference (RGF §8; PD §7;
RT, RC, RL failure lists).

1. **Streaks, daily counters, absence penalties** — Duolingo streak research (J. Consumer Research 2023) and ACM L@S 2022 show fixation on the mechanic over the activity; a group meets every two
   or three weeks.
2. **A narrator that speaks uninvited and never learns** — Clippy "optimized for first use"
   (Stanford, press); push tutorials are skipped and do not improve performance (NN/g, PD E4 §1).
3. **Relayouts that trade genre flavour for generic minimalism** — the 2026 D&D Beyond thread
   ("sterile, empty, and zero D&D flavor"); Foundry's window clutter needing PopOut!/Minimal UI
   modules; every narrated card that opens a window is a window someone must close.
4. **Unskippable rituals and animation fatigue** — Larian forum requests to skip dice animations;
   Dice So Nice ships 2x/3x and auto-hide for the same reason; Veilguard's overstimulating motion
   with no calm setting (PRI failures).
5. **Confetti on every action** — UX Collective and UX Planet: celebrate what the user wanted, at
   milestones, never on save.
6. **Displayed numbers diverging from simulated ones** — Baldur's Gate 3 tooltips wrong for months
   while the roll maths stayed right (PD §7 item 2); one source of truth, one test.
7. **Maths shown but not applied** — D&D Beyond's short-rest panel states what each hit die returns
   and still demands manual HP entry (PD §7 item 3).
8. **A tooltip layer that dies where lookup matters** — D&D Beyond's `[tag]` tooltips disabled on
   the sheet (PD §7 item 1); Italian compendium with English tooltips (RL failure).
9. **Permanence by omission and a step list that implies an order it does not enforce** — BG3 race lock and its "overwhelming" complaint (PD §7 item 4).
10. **Question-phrased dialogs and inverted action labels** — dnd5e removed question-mark rest
    dialogs; its chat card where "Apply Healing" dealt damage (RT failures 1, 11).
11. **Hidden preconditions for sharing** — D&D Beyond's three invisible switches behind "my players
    cannot see my homebrew" (RL failures).
12. **Gamification without a learner-centred purpose** — NN/g: scoring social life feels wrong; too
    hard → helplessness, too easy → boredom.

## 4. The checklist Astra's screens are reviewed against

1. Framing (G1): one in-world sentence per step and per domain root.
2. Identity (G2): name before numbers; every later title uses it.
3. Preview (G3): numeric delta before every commit; the same numbers in the receipt.
4. Register (G4): the four checks on every string; empty states with cue and reason.
5. Feedback (G5): settle after trigger; crit/fumble states; value fixed before animation; every
   juice element removable without changing a number.
6. Budget (G6): four tagged budgets; reduced-motion and the fast preference collapse the last two.
7. Bypass (G7): skip, remembered switch, Recommended on any step with more than three choices.
8. Rituals (G8): four roll modes; level-up as invitation; combat from the table phrase; crits from
   the rules.
9. Failures (§3): none of the twelve reappears.
